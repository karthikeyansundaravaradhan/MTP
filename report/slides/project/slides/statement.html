Problem statement

Detect drowsiness in unseen drivers

91,000

police-reported US crashes involving drowsy drivers in 2017, with about 800 deaths (NHTSA)

2024

drowsiness and attention warning mandatory in every new EU vehicle (Regulation 2019/2144)

The task

Input: frontal IR face video of a new driver, plus vehicle telemetry when available

Output: KSS level every 60 s: Alert (≤3), Low-vigilant (4–6) or Drowsy (≥7)

Constraints: subject-independent, camera-based (no wearables), runs on a 16 GB laptop, keeps working if the camera fails

Questions: what is an honest score on UL-DD, can it survive sensor loss, and what does the score actually measure?

2

Drowsy driving causes tens of thousands of crashes a year, and camera-based driver monitoring is now required in new European vehicles. The task we set is concrete. Given the frontal infrared video of a driver the model has never seen, plus telemetry when it is available, predict the three-class KSS level for every 60 second window. It must be camera-based, run on a laptop, and degrade gracefully when the camera fails. Three questions follow: what an honest score looks like on UL-DD, whether the system survives sensor loss, and what the score really measures.
